8 · Am I Doing This Right? Self-Check Guide

Practicing alone without an instructor watching is the hardest part for most beginners — there is no one to tell you if your form has drifted. This page collects the five most common beginner patterns from Chapter 4, each with a quick way to check yourself and a simple fix, so you can catch and correct these on your own.

MistakeHow to tellFix
Locked kneesGently bounce your knees a half-inch up and down — if they will not move freely, they are locked.Unlock them and let them settle into a soft, slightly bent position.
Rushing the weight shiftCount slowly to four as you shift from one leg to the other — if you finish before four, you are moving too fast.Slow down further than feels natural at first; that is normal.
Looking down at your feetNotice if your head keeps tipping forward to check foot placement during movement.Trust your feet. To check placement, pause completely, look, adjust, then lift your gaze back to level.
Holding your breathNotice if you are holding your breath, especially during arm-raising movements.Exhale consciously and let the next breath happen naturally — no need to force a pattern.
Overcommitting the weight shiftCheck if your empty foot lifts fully off the ground or your hip juts out to the side.Aim for the 70/30 proportion — a clear shift, not a full transfer. Both feet stay in light contact with the floor.
